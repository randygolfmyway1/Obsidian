## One-sentence promise (say this up top)

> Commercial insurance has been around for hundreds of years. Most of the industry was indoctrinated to sell better price and better coverage. Tonight we dig out **your philosophy** — then build the **story that exposes your prospect’s false belief** — so you can position against the incumbent in a way they can’t match with one phone call.

## Teach: Philosophy is not a USP slogan (3 min)

**Kill the abstract USP question.**  
> “What’s your unique selling proposition? … It’s a conceptual question and all you’ll get is a conceptual answer… the exact same thing your competition would say.” — *LIES ABOUT DIFFERENTIATION*

**Philosophy = the truth you accept that forces a process.**  
If you don’t accept a hard truth, you keep improvising quotes.

**Three of Randy’s philosophies (model only — they write their own):**

| Philosophy (truth we accept) | What it forces us to build |
|------------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Hiring / GRIT:** Don’t ask if they’re capable — make them *do* things so evidence shows capability. | Evidence-based interview / prove-it process (*Grit*) |
| **Wedge:** Somebody has to lose for you to win. Zero-sum. Incumbent must lose. | Process that gets the prospect to see the incumbent isn’t doing the job |
| **Sales meetings:** Most meetings are a “spreadsheet liars club.” | Put a producer on the hot seat: **Define → Defend → Develop** how they’re better than the incumbent |

**Quote for sales meetings:**  
> “Second part is what I call the spreadsheet liars club… That’s not a sales meeting. That’s activity theater.”  
> “There is no such thing as your producer winning an account without the incumbent losing it.”  
> Hot seat: “Define what you do better… Defend how you do it better… Develop… build your wedges.” — *90 Minutes, 12 Producers*

**Belief chain (why philosophy matters):**  
> “Experience drives our beliefs. Our beliefs then drive our actions… actions… results.” — *Experience – Beliefs – Actions – Results*

Their philosophy is the **belief they want the prospect to leave with** — after a story that challenges the prospect’s current belief.

## Core exercise: The 5-box story (12–15 min)

Have them fill the worksheet. Walk box by box. Don’t let them write “better service.”

### Box 1 — Your philosophy (one hard sentence)

Prompt:  
*What truth do you accept that most agents in this industry refuse to accept?*

Bad: “We care more.” / “We’re local.” / “Great carriers.”  
Good pattern: “We believe X is true, therefore we refuse to do Y, and we always do Z.”

Randy model lines (read aloud, then they write theirs):  
- “Somebody has to lose for you to win — so we build the call around getting the prospect to see the income that’s not doing their job.”  
- “We don’t ask salespeople if they’re capable — we make them do things so we know.”  
- “A sales meeting that only reviews a spreadsheet doesn’t make anyone better — so we put producers on the hot seat.”

### Box 2 — Prospect’s false belief

Prompt:  
*What does your ideal prospect already believe that keeps them with a mediocre incumbent?*

Common false beliefs (offer as menu, not their only options):  
- “My guy has us covered; renewal is just paperwork.”  
- “If price and coverage look similar, staying is safer.”  
- “Agents are interchangeable; the carrier is what matters.”  
- “I’ll know it’s time to switch when something blows up.”  
- “Proactive service is just marketing language.”

**Anchor quote:**  
> Buyers hear “We provide excellent service… We really care…” — “The prospect had heard those exact words from three agents this month… They’re meaningless… because they’re unverifiable.” — *Why You Lose 70% of Deals*

### Box 3 — The story that makes the false belief visible

Prompt:  
*Tell a short story (not a pitch) where someone held that false belief — and what it cost them. Prefer a client/prospect pattern you’ve seen, not a fairy tale.*

Structure (60–90 seconds when spoken):  
1. Who / situation  
2. What they believed  
3. What they couldn’t see  
4. What broke (or almost broke)  
5. What they now refuse to believe

If they freeze: use **industry pattern story** (incumbent uses your proposal as a shopping list) — from *Why You Lose 70% of Deals* — then personalize with a niche they sell.

### Box 4 — The transition (belief flip)

Prompt:  
*One sentence that moves them from false belief → your philosophy.*

Pattern:  
“So the question isn’t who’s a little cheaper this year — it’s whether you want a broker who reacts at renewal, or one who runs a written plan you can hold them to.”

**Blueprint line (quote):**  
> “If someone were going to build a house, would they insist on having a blueprint first? … Have you ever had a blueprint for how your insurance and risk management program was going to be managed?” — *Why You Lose 70% of Deals*

### Box 5 — Positioning against the competition

Prompt:  
*Name 3–5 concrete things you do that the incumbent typically doesn’t — named, dated, deliverable.*

**Quote:**  
> “Difference is really in three categories: price, coverage, and service… Pricing and coverage [are] controlled… by the insurance carrier… The thing you control is service.” — *Your Differentiation Matters*

**Real game:**  
> Convert capabilities into “separate individual and distinct proactive services” with name, deliverable, result you can measure/monetize. — *LIES ABOUT DIFFERENTIATION*

Hot-seat test (say it):  
If you can’t **define and defend** it in this room, you won’t sell it in theirs.

---

## Live hot seat (10–12 min)

Pick 2–3 volunteers. Same rhythm as Chris sales meeting:

1. **Define** — philosophy in one sentence  
2. **Defend** — walk the false belief → story → transition  
3. **Develop** — 3 wedges / proactive services the incumbent isn’t doing  

Coach language:  
- “Too abstract — give me the deliverable and the date.”  
- “That’s price/coverage — incumbent matches that with one call.”  
- “What’s the false belief you’re killing?”  
- “Would the prospect think: *I never looked at it that way. My guy’s not doing that?*”

---

## Close (2 min)

**Recap:**  
Philosophy (truth) → Story (false belief) → Transition → Concrete services only you run.

**Charge:**  
Before your next sales call, fill the 5 boxes for **one named account**. Bring it to your next sales meeting hot seat.

**Optional CTA:**  
Wedge / Bignition / written service timeline — only if it fits the room.

**Sign-off line:**  
> Keep busting the incumbent relationship.

## Facilitator cheat sheet — Randy examples only

**GRIT hiring philosophy:** Evidence over answers. Make candidates perform so you know capability (*Grit*).  

**Wedge philosophy:** Win-lose. Accept it; build process so prospect sees the incumbent’s gap.  

**Sales meeting philosophy:** Spreadsheet liars club vs hot seat (Define / Defend / Develop).  

**Differentiation philosophy:** Old game = price/coverage/service → rolled. New game = sell capabilities → buyer confused. Real game = named proactive services you can compare.  

**Psychology:** Experience → Beliefs → Actions → Results — tonight you give them a new *belief* experience so they act differently on the next call.
